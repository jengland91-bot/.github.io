Why this layout

The Plus is a great sim button box — eight LCD keys, a touch strip, four encoders — but it ships as a desk wedge. This kit does two things at once: a faceplate that covers the Elgato bezel so it reads as a panel, and a mount that uses the same M8 T-nuts 6 Sigma already puts in the chassis.

The clamp is a U-channel around a 40 mm section, so it fits 4040, a 40 mm face of 4080 / 40120, the shifter arm, or a wheel-deck upright. On a vertical bar the hinge swings the deck toward you. On a horizontal bar it nods the viewing angle. Mirror the cradle and clamp in the slicer for the left side.

Put it together

  1. Print the SVG at 100% or print fit_gauge.stl. Lay it on the Plus. Openings should clear the keys, strip, and dials.
  2. Pull the four dial caps straight off — they are designed to come off.
  3. Remove the two Phillips screws in the stand. Keep them.
  4. Drop the faceplate on, then press the caps back. The caps retain the plate.
  5. Seat the Plus in the cradle with USB-C toward the hinge slot. Optionally run the M3 screws through the floor slots. Zip-tie if you want a belt.
  6. Slide the clamp onto a 40 mm face of the chassis. Two T-nuts, two M8s.
  7. Bolt cradle to clamp with the M5. Aim the face at your hand, snug the nylock.

Good spots on a 6 Sigma

  • Shifter / handbrake arm — the usual button-box place, right of the wheel.
  • Wheel-deck side upright, inboard face, so the Plus looks at you.
  • A spare 4040 you add as an accessory rail.

If a hole is tight

Cutouts are based on Elgato’s published 140 × 138 × 110 mm body (with stand), the 108 × 14 mm touch strip, and M3 × 8 stand screws. Key pitch, dial diameter, housing depth, and M3 spacing are estimated on purpose, then given clearance. If the gauge is off, edit cad/params.py and regenerate: 

python3 cad/generate.py

Needs Python 3 and numpy. See README.md for the full write-up.
